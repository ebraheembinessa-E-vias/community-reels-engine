# First, clean your recording.
Route A: your voice and meaning stay yours.

## What you provide
Your original recording, who it is for and the one message you want people to remember. Tell your assistant what must stay. If you do not know what needs cutting, say so.

## What your assistant does
It understands the whole take, finds repeated attempts and awkward gaps, and keeps the version that finishes your thought. It checks actual speech at each cut so quiet word endings survive. Your original stays separate.

## What you will see
A clean preview **before animation**, with corrected caption wording. The assistant explains the meaningful changes in simple language.

## What you check
Play it with sound. Does the opening make sense? Are the joins natural? Is any word cut off? Does it still sound like you? Are names and technical words correct?

> Useful feedback: "The jump around 9 seconds is too sharp." Or: "Keep the example about customers." Or: "That caption is not what I said."

When it is right, say: "The clean cut and the words are right. Continue." Your assistant should wait for your review of this actual version before adding visual elements. Approval of an idea is not approval of an unseen edit.

# Then, make it clearer.
Route A: choose visuals that help the sentence land.

Your assistant asks what would help the viewer understand. It recommends a style and shows a small example before finishing the whole reel.

## Captions and emphasis
Readable words timed to your actual speech. Natural phrasing, correct spelling and space for the phone app's controls. Tell it which language or dialect you want.

## Explanations and comparisons
A process map, two options beside each other, a short list, a real screen capture or a product image. Each visual should explain the point you are making.

## Your look and assets
Provide your colors, logo, screenshots or a reference if you have them. If you do not, ask for a clean starter style. Your face should remain visible when it is part of the story.

> Try: "Keep it calm. Use our real screenshot when I explain the result. Show the three steps as I mention them. I want useful animation and readable captions."

Your assistant should not invent proof, tool logos, customer results or a promised download. If it needs a real asset, it should tell you exactly which one and why.

Watch the animated preview with sound. If the words or cuts need changing, return to the clean version. If only the picture needs work, ask for a visual revision.

# Start with an idea.
Route B: a faceless reel begins with a conversation.

> Your first message can be: "I want a faceless reel, but I only have an idea." Your assistant should help you narrow it down.

## The conversation
First it asks who you want to help. Then it finds the one useful point. It asks about language, tone, length and what the viewer should do next. These are conversation topics, **not a form to complete all at once**.

If you are unsure, ask it to recommend a focused first idea and explain why. It should remember answers you have already given.

## The script and scene plan
It drafts an opening, a useful explanation and a clear ending. Beside each part, it describes what will appear and why. Ask it to sound like a person talking to another person. Review the words before narration or paid assets are made.

## The sound
Choose your own recorded voice, an artificial narrator if an authorized tool is available, or a deliberate text-only reel. Your assistant explains the process and cost. You check the voice and actual words; for text-only, you check reading time.

## The animation
It builds scenes around the measured narration or intentional text timing, then shows you the animated result. It should not guess caption timing or leave unexplained empty sections.

The included engine imports audio or builds a deliberately silent story from a plan. Artificial narration is an optional, separately authorized step using a tool available to your assistant. It does not secretly replace your voice.

# Find your style. Then review.
A reference helps your assistant understand what you like.

You can provide a reel and say: "I like the pace and the way it explains each idea." The reference decoder studies those choices and proposes an original version for your message.

> Try: "Explain how this reference opens, uses captions and moves between ideas. Then suggest a version for my audience. Tell me which real assets we need."

## Five starting styles
**VOX:** the designer's own paper-documentary style, your footage as a taped polaroid on a warm desk. **Editorial:** a calm explanation. **Signal:** one focused point. **Diagram:** connected steps. **Pulse:** a faceless series where a signal travels through your steps on a dark canvas. You can ask for a small preview; you do not need to choose fonts or write animation instructions yourself.

## Watch it like your audience
Does the opening make sense immediately? Is there one clear point? Do captions match the speech? Is text readable on a phone? Are images honest and useful? Does the ending give one clear next step?

> Ask for a precise revision: "Make the first three seconds clearer." "Let that diagram stay longer." "The narrator sounds stiff; make it warmer." "Keep the message, but simplify the background."

When you approve, your assistant gives you the final video and tells you where it is saved. It should save the brief and your decisions so you can continue later.

You choose when and where to publish. The engine does not post automatically or guarantee views. If a tool fails, your assistant should say what is finished and what still needs work.

# Assistant: the recording route.
Preserve meaning, complete words and the creator's own voice.

Use **community-clean** and **docs/workflow.md**. Prepare a new project and preserve the original. Understand the whole authorized recording, correct the transcript against actual audio, identify repeated attempts and measure each join using replay and waveform evidence. Model times or a silence threshold are not word-accurate truth.

Show the clean video and corrected captions. Explain meaningful removals. Record approval only after the creator approves those actual artifacts. If speech changes later, rebuild and repeat review. A technical check or synthetic fixture does not establish creator acceptance.

Use **community-elements** for the visual pass. Ask what would help the audience understand. Recommend one style and show a small preview. Choose elements by purpose: compare, connect, demonstrate, explain or invite. Use real screenshots and official tool logos when relevant, with provenance. Keep faces, captions and phone-safe areas readable.

> Technical path: prepare, analyze, correct transcript, measure edit plan, clean, creator review, approve, dress, render, final review.

The assistant operates these steps. The person should not need to write JSON or run commands. Read current command help and docs/workflow.md for exact formats. Existing authorization for the same upload and budget remains valid.

For mixed Arabic/English captions, preserve shaped Arabic phrases and intact Latin product names. Do not animate separate Arabic letters. Recheck the final word, entrances, holds and exits rather than inspecting only a cover frame.

# Assistant: the faceless route.
The person may arrive with no recording and only an idea.

Use **docs/faceless.md**. Turn the interview into a brief and concise script: opening, useful body and one next action. Present a plain-language scene plan alongside it. Verify factual claims. Get script approval before narration or paid assets.

Ask for a voice choice: their own recording, an authorized artificial narrator, or intentional silence. Use a capability actually available in their environment. Google TTS is separate from video analysis. Preview generated speech and verify every word. Do not silently replace a provider or clone a real person's voice.

Measure actual narration and caption intervals; do not estimate onsets from word counts. For text-only, review reading time. Write the story plan yourself, with actual script approval and purposeful visuals. **story** imports narration or prepares silence; it does not generate a voice.

```text
python3 reel.py story work/story.json --name my-story --audio work/voice.wav
# Use --silent instead of --audio only when intentionally text-only.
# After the person reviews and approves the master and captions:
python3 reel.py approve my-story --by "Creator name"
python3 reel.py dress my-story --style diagram --faceless
python3 reel.py render my-story --style diagram --faceless
```

The plain-background story master is an audio/text checkpoint before animation. Show it with its captions; do not describe it as a finished reel. Review the final animation at normal speed and fix empty visual gaps or mismatched narration.
